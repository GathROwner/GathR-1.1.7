# GathR Social Media Launch Plan

This plan is for reserving and refreshing GathR social accounts before launch while keeping public claims conservative. It assumes the app is still pre-launch or early-launch and should avoid promising coverage, venue participation, or features that are not already live.

## Positioning

GathR helps people find local things to do without checking dozens of venue pages. The strongest initial angle is practical discovery: events, specials, live music, trivia, food and drink, and other nearby plans in one place.

Primary audience:

- People who want quick weekend or same-day plans
- Locals who follow restaurants, bars, venues, and community pages
- Visitors who want a simple way to find what is happening nearby
- Venue owners and event organizers who want more visibility

Core message:

> Stop scrolling for plans. GathR shows what is happening nearby.

Supporting messages:

- Find events, specials, and nights out in one local feed.
- Discover live music, trivia, food and drink, community events, and more.
- Spend less time searching and more time going.

## Account Strategy

Use the same handle wherever possible:

- Preferred: `@gathrapp`
- Backup: `@trygathr`
- Backup: `@gathr.events`
- Backup: `@gathr.local`

Use the same profile image across all channels. If the app icon is already final, use that. If it is not final, use a simple GathR wordmark or temporary launch mark rather than changing the visual identity repeatedly.

Recommended link target:

- If the app store pages are not live: a simple landing page, waitlist, or Linktree-style page
- If the app store pages are live: the app store link first, then support/contact links

## Platform Priorities

### Facebook

Goal: Credibility, local reach, community discovery, and venue/event organizer trust.

Use Facebook for:

- Page presence and searchability
- Event discovery posts
- Local group sharing, only where allowed
- Venue and organizer outreach
- Launch announcements

Facebook should feel slightly more informational than trendy. Keep posts specific and useful.

### Instagram

Goal: Visual discovery and local lifestyle awareness.

Use Instagram for:

- Reels showing "what to do this weekend"
- Carousel posts featuring event categories
- Stories with polls, countdowns, and local prompts
- Screenshots or screen recordings once the app experience is polished

Instagram should show the actual app experience, real local context, and useful plans. Avoid generic stock-style filler.

### TikTok

Goal: Lightweight discovery content and fast awareness testing.

Use TikTok for:

- Quick "things to do near you" videos
- Screen recording style demos
- Local weekend roundup formats
- Founder-style explanations if they are natural and low effort

TikTok should be simple and direct. Do not overproduce early posts before learning which format gets traction.

## Setup Checklist

### Facebook

- Create or refresh the GathR Facebook Page.
- Confirm the page name, handle, profile image, and cover image.
- Add the short description and website/waitlist link.
- Fill in contact details only where they are ready for public use.

### Instagram

- Create or reserve the handle.
- Add the profile image, bio, category, and link.
- Post 3 launch-grid posts before inviting broader follows.
- Add Story Highlights only when there is enough content to support them.
- Use Reels early, but keep the format simple and repeatable.

### TikTok

- Create or reserve the handle.
- Add the profile image, bio, and link if available.
- Post 2 to 3 test videos using different hooks.
- Track which hook performs best before making polished versions.
- Avoid trend-dependent posts unless they still explain GathR clearly.

## Manual Review Guardrails

Do not publish claims that are not already true in the app or launch market.

Avoid:

- "Every event in your city"
- "All local venues"
- "Never miss an event again"
- "Official partner of" unless a partnership exists
- Exact launch dates unless the date is approved

Use safer language:

- "Built to help you find..."
- "Coming soon..."
- "Local events and specials..."
- "More details soon..."
- "Launching first in select areas..."

## Launch Sequence

1. Reserve handles and make profiles visually consistent.
2. Add conservative profile copy and the best available link.
3. Publish 2 to 3 foundational posts.
4. Add a short app demo once the product flow is stable.
5. Invite early supporters and testers.
6. Start weekly local discovery posts.
7. Announce launch only after the app store or signup path is ready.

## Notes For Codex Work

This plan is intentionally documentation-only. Social account publishing, login-based setup, and profile changes should be done manually by the account owner or through an authenticated browser session with explicit approval.
